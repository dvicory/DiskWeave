## Context

Current canonical requirements already own identity/topology, store capability and persistence evidence, crash-releasing writer claims, recovery writable-open and conservative commit observations, operation-slot lifetime and generation reuse, checksum-owner evidence, range-basis vocabulary, and operator publication. A reviewed durable writable-session candidate has been recovered separately, but it remains an active external target until it is integrated, reviewed in this lineage, and canonicalized. The missing startup fact is bounded physical no-late behavior across a custody gap. The file-backed/store ownership boundary allocates and revalidates the exact claim and stable backing-resource/alias binding; the store-operation boundary owns the physical no-late fact because it can establish predecessor physical-reachability/closure within the selected capability/profile's declared and evidence-supported physical reachability universe while that binding remains held.

This change keeps current behavior distinct from its target: current canonical requirements are unchanged; these deltas remain proposed until the normal review, implementation, verification, sync, and archive flow. A complete present member set means a stable, unambiguous assignment and bounded availability observation for every required member. It does not mean every payload byte has been scanned or proven readable, correct, current, or historically continuous.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals:**

- Define one scan-independent baseline writable-startup admission for a complete stable assignment set after a custody gap.
- Make `store-operation-contracts` the sole canonical producer of the physical no-late admission fact.
- Require allocation and continuous holding of the opaque exact current claim token and stable backing-resource/alias binding before stabilization, with current claim/incarnation/ordering-domain binding, the selected capability/profile's declared and evidence-supported physical reachability universe, exact predecessor reachability/closure within that universe, and a monotonic boundary. Revalidate that binding and continuously hold/revalidate writable-recovery authority after every pre-commit checksum, range-role basis, coded-range-authority, recovery `CLEAN`, session, and epoch gate through the epoch commit and observation of its result; healthy service and operator start independently continue those authority checks from that observation through admission return, frontend handoff, and publication.
- Make `success`, `unsupported`, `failed`, and `uncertain` outcomes, interruption/retry posture, and all-or-nothing required-store aggregation explicit.
- Make known refusal, unsupported capability, operational failure, and reconciliation-required uncertainty distinguishable through existing result dimensions.
- Preserve owner-local claims, failure meanings, range-local basis, and non-claims.

**Non-goals:**

- No new identity, topology, writer-lease, store-persistence, operation-slot, transaction-release, session-lifecycle, coded-range-authority, recovery `CLEAN`, checksum, basis, currentization, or frontend-publication owner.
- No payload enumeration, parity recomputation, checksum revalidation, historical-continuity proof, per-operation history ledger, payload scan, historical compaction, background rollover, strict-startup profile, or affected-claim retirement policy.
- No executor, runtime, scheduler, lock implementation, queue, database, schema, batching, Linux transport, or implementation decomposition.
- No model in this planning head. The model gate is described below; an evidence-only profile is not a semantic owner.

## Decisions

### 1. Admission consumes owner facts and has one semantic order
Startup first performs read-only recovery inspection and current identity/topology validation. It then asks the file-backed/store ownership owner to reacquire every required store claim and exact resource/alias binding, and reacquires writable-recovery authority as one current premise, with no pre-begin session authority. The reviewed `define-portable-writable-session-lifecycle` target supplies the durable session-begin transition only after its normal authority transition; it is not current canonical authority in this packet. A known partial claim failure requires the file-backed/store owner to provide its current definite release-all result for every acquired partial lease and the writable-recovery owner to provide its separate disposition. If file-backed release fails or is unknown, startup preserves that owner failure or uncertainty, blocks pending a separately reconciled file-backed-owner semantic prerequisite, and performs no retry or reacquisition. An unknown or mismatched partial acquisition is reconciliation-required: it is not treated as no claim, and startup does not silently retry or begin a second session.

The semantic order is:

1. Inspect recovery and envelope state without initialization, migration, or repair.
2. Re-observe policy locators, identity, topology, assignments, geometry, capability, and recovery health.
3. Ask the file-backed/store ownership owner to acquire all required opaque exact current claim tokens and stable-resource/alias bindings, and acquire recovery authority under the same captured premise.
4. For each required store, establish `AdmissionStabilizationEvidence` while its exact current claim token and stable-resource/alias binding and the writable-recovery authority are continuously held. Each successful result binds current claim/incarnation/domain and selected capability/profile separately from its exact predecessor physical reachability/closure scope within that profile's declared universe and proves only the store-scoped physical no-late property for that scope, not persistence or durability.
5. Aggregate successful, correctly bound stabilization evidence for every required store. The set is all-or-nothing; one store cannot compensate for another.
6. Consume checksum-owner evidence compared under its exact target/range, topology epoch, digest profile, checksum-set generation, target content generation, and persistence-evidence bindings; consume the recovery-produced range-role basis snapshot bound to exact recovery generation/topology epoch/role/range/source evidence; and consume each exact coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` owner observation as a separately named prerequisite without combining or relabeling them, deriving a startup-owned input or claim, reconstructing membership or capture policy, or claiming `CLEAN`. Freshly revalidate every claim/resource/alias binding and writable-recovery authority after these gates.
7. Invoke the reviewed lifecycle target for durable writable-session begin while retaining and revalidating every exact claim/resource/alias binding and continuously holding/revalidating writable-recovery authority; acquire no second session authority and do not treat this active target as current canonical authority.
8. Ask recovery to durably commit a new protection epoch bound to all preceding exact owner facts, recovery-produced basis snapshot, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` observations, and revalidated bindings while the exact store and writable-recovery authorities remain held and freshly revalidated through observation of the commit result.
9. Have healthy service consume the observed epoch result, then independently freshly revalidate every claim/resource/alias binding and writable-recovery authority before returning the bounded admission candidate; this post-commit gate preserves durable session/epoch facts but does not rewrite or roll back them if later publication cannot proceed.
10. Invoke the selected frontend only when its separately owned publication, currentization, and basis-action gates pass, with the healthy service/operator independently holding and freshly revalidating every claim/resource/alias binding and writable-recovery authority from observed epoch commit through admission return, handoff, and successful endpoint publication.

The order is a semantic dependency and linearized premise, not a lock, thread, or syscall prescription. Store stabilization must follow file-backed/store ownership acquisition of the claim/resource binding; recovery must hold and freshly revalidate writable-recovery authority through the epoch commit and observation of its result only. Healthy service and operator start then independently continue holding and freshly revalidating the exact store and writable-recovery authorities from that observed commit through admission return, frontend handoff, and successful publication. Loss, replacement, remap, alias change, release, or reacquisition invalidates the applicable evidence and fails closed at the owning boundary; loss after a durable epoch commit blocks later admission/publication without erasing or rolling back durable session/epoch facts. For a known partial multi-store acquisition failure, the file-backed/store owner supplies its current definite release-all result for every acquired partial lease. For a full-set store-authority release or any later owner-gate failure, this packet does not assume a release-all result: startup preserves the file-backed owner’s ownership and outcome, blocks pending the separately reconciled file-backed-owner semantic prerequisite, and performs no inferred release, retry, or reacquisition. Any failed or unknown file-backed release likewise remains unresolved under that prerequisite. The writable-recovery owner separately owns its authority's release, retention, or reconciliation disposition.

### 2. The store owner establishes the physical no-late boundary

The store-operation boundary SHALL be the single canonical producer of cross-custody physical no-late admission evidence. The file-backed/store ownership owner SHALL solely acquire the exact current writer claim or equivalent exclusion and exact stable backing-resource/alias binding for the target store, exposing only an opaque claim token to consumers. That owner SHALL keep the claim/resource binding continuously held while the observation is established, while evidence is consumed, and through every later premise revalidation. The evidence SHALL bind the exact current claim token, current store incarnation, current ordering-domain identity, stable backing-resource identity and alias set, selected capability/profile and its declared and evidence-supported physical reachability universe, assignment slot and instance, assignment generation, protected geometry, topology epoch, monotonic stabilization boundary, and exact predecessor physical-reachability/closure scope within that universe. The writable-recovery owner is separately responsible for continuously holding and freshly revalidating its authority through all of those boundaries; store stabilization does not allocate or release that authority.

For that exact current binding, `success` means that every covered pre-boundary effect in the exact scope within the selected profile's declared physical reachability universe has completed or ceased before the boundary, so none can mutate the stable backing resource after the boundary. This is physical quiescence only: it is not a persistence fence and does not prove that already-visible bytes reached persistent media. A new store incarnation, process loss, lease acquisition, or a fence limited to the current ordering domain is insufficient. A later successful boundary under the continuously held exact claim/resource binding covers at least the physical routes/effects covered by an earlier successful boundary, without covering effects initiated after the named boundary. The boundary is comparable only inside its exact current claim/incarnation/domain, selected profile/universe, and stable resource/alias scope; it is not wall-clock time, a guessed timestamp, an accepted-write watermark, or a cross-store fence. An unknown, unattributed, untracked, or uncertifiable route inside the selected universe makes the result unsupported or uncertain. Routes and actors explicitly outside that universe remain profile assumptions and non-claims; stabilization does not silently broaden the profile.

The result has exactly one disposition: `success`, `unsupported`, `failed`, or `uncertain`. `unsupported` means the bound store/profile cannot identify and close, or otherwise certify closure of, every capable physical route/effect required by its declared universe. `failed` means the attempt definitely did not establish that closure. `uncertain` means interruption, disappearance, timeout, claim loss, resource replacement/remap, alias-set change, incomplete in-profile physical-source coverage, or an unclassifiable observation prevents proving whether it was established. None supplies success. An interrupted attempt remains uncertain unless the store owner independently establishes a successful exact observation. A later attempt must revalidate the current claim/resource/alias binding, reacquire and continuously hold the exact then-current binding, and produce fresh evidence with a fresh boundary; startup does not automatically upgrade or reuse uncertain evidence.

Writer-claim acquisition alone is insufficient: crash-releasing exclusion prevents a concurrent current writer but does not prove that effects issued through predecessor or unattributed routes within the selected profile's declared universe have completed or ceased. Recovery or operation history alone is insufficient: it preserves logical intent, identity, disposition, or uncertainty but does not observe that old kernel/device queues, DMA, raw/out-of-band paths, aliases, remaps, or other physical effects included by that universe have stopped reaching the stable backing resource. The store observation must account for every route/effect within that universe capable of reaching the resource, including still-owned in-process children when their effects are in scope, while leaving operation outcome, terminality, reconciliation, slot reclamation, transaction release, durability, and persistence to their existing owners.

`evidence_strength` is opaque provenance only. It has no local policy effect beyond exact-binding checks, mismatch detection, and non-transfer, and cannot select, upgrade, or downgrade a profile unless another named owner defines that policy. Admission evidence is specific to the opaque exact current claim token, current incarnation, current ordering domain, stable backing-resource/alias binding, selected capability/profile and declared physical reachability universe, and startup premise. Predecessor physical closure is coverage supporting only that bound stabilization result; it does not transfer current authority to or from any predecessor source. The file-backed/store ownership owner freshly revalidates its binding after each canonical owner gate, including checksum, range-role basis, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` observations, session, and epoch, and at frontend handoff/publication; writable-recovery authority is revalidated at the same boundaries. Any loss, remap, alias change, release, or reacquisition invalidates the evidence and fails closed.

### 3. Stabilization has narrow claims and preserves independent owners

Successful `AdmissionStabilizationEvidence` proves only the bounded physical no-late fact. It does not prove operation outcome, persistence or durability, payload readability or correctness, parity agreement, checksum validity, recovery `CLEAN`, lineage, custody continuity, range basis, `Current`, current protection, operation-slot reclamation, transaction release, or any other owner state transition. A store result cannot manufacture any of those claims, and startup cannot broaden the result while projecting admission.

Current in-process children, resources, completions, and reconciliation remain owned by `OperationSlotTable` and its related owners. Store stabilization respects an effect that can still reach the stable backing resource, including unattributed physical routes, but does not derive child terminality, release, transaction completion, or generic handoff. No per-operation history is required to survive reboot merely to make the physical no-late observation valid; an operation no longer physically capable of reaching the resource is covered by the exact physical predecessor closure, while any live owner fact remains with its owner.

### 4. Recovery owns the durable epoch transition

Recovery owns creation of the fresh protection-epoch identity, generation-checked durable commit, and prior-versus-proposed restart interpretation. The epoch record binds accepted topology and assignments, current writer/recovery authority identities, writable-recovery authority held and freshly revalidated through the epoch commit and observation of its result, successful freshly revalidated exact stabilization for every required store, checksum-owner evidence compared under exact target/range, topology epoch, digest profile, checksum-set generation, target content generation, and persistence-evidence bindings, the recovery-produced range-role basis snapshot bound to exact recovery generation/topology epoch/role/range/source evidence, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` owner observations, the durable session-begin result from the reviewed lifecycle target, and recovery generation. Recovery does not construct checksum, basis vocabulary, coded membership, CLEAN capture, capture frontier, future exclusion, or store stabilization internals.
 
A known epoch rejection after durable session begin leaves the prior epoch authoritative and preserves the separate session fact. A lost, corrupt, or unclassifiable epoch outcome preserves prior/proposed evidence, publishes nothing, and requires owner reconciliation; it does not authorize a second epoch, automatic retry, rollback of session begin, or a current claim. If canonical checksum evidence, the recovery basis source evidence, the coded-range-authority observation, or the recovery `CLEAN` capture/state is rejected or uncertain, recovery preserves each owner result separately and creates no epoch. For known partial multi-store acquisition failure, the file-backed/store owner provides its current definite release-all result for every acquired partial lease. For a full-set store-authority release or any later owner-gate failure, no release-all result is assumed: startup preserves the file-backed owner’s ownership and outcome, blocks pending the separately reconciled file-backed-owner prerequisite, and performs no inferred release, retry, or reacquisition; writable-recovery authority separately provides its release, retention, or reconciliation disposition. Range-role basis classifications remain exact: `current` names the active epoch/range/participating generations/committed durable transition, `prior` the exact earlier protected generation, `unprotected` only an affirmative source-owner observation, and indeterminate/not-yet-interpretable remain so.

### 5. Admission and publication remain separate

Healthy service returns a bounded admission object containing stable assignment bindings, opaque current claim/resource/alias bindings, exact per-store physical no-late evidence and predecessor scopes, canonical checksum-owner evidence under its exact comparison dimensions, the recovery-produced range-role basis snapshot and its exact source bindings, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` owner observations, session-begin evidence from the reviewed lifecycle target, writable-recovery authority held and freshly revalidated from the observed epoch commit through admission return, epoch identity, allowed claims, non-claims, and owner/cause information. It does not return a public endpoint or unconditional protected-write authority.

Operator start may invoke the frontend only after durable session and epoch admission has been observed, canonical checksum-owner evidence, the recovery-produced range-role basis snapshot, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` owner observations, and final successful claim/resource/alias revalidation. It must independently hold and revalidate every claim/resource/alias binding and writable-recovery authority from the observed epoch commit through admission return, again at handoff, and through endpoint publication. If publication is unsupported or fails, start preserves durable session/epoch evidence and the frontend owner's next action. It does not roll back, auto-close, auto-release, auto-rebegin, or report the array online. Admission validity remains distinguishable from publication failure.

### 6. Model and evidence boundary

No new executable model is added in this planning slice. Existing models own other bounded decisions, while the exact startup transition depends on owner observations that are not all present or canonically owned in this workspace lineage, notably the external writable-session target, the separate coded-range-authority observation, the separate recovery `CLEAN` capture/state observation, and the unresolved file-backed-owner release prerequisite. Adding a partial model now would create shadow lifecycle, ownership, basis, or release semantics and could not establish correspondence for those external inputs.

After prerequisite owner identities are integrated, a bounded recovery-owned protection-epoch model is warranted only if the exact session/epoch transition remains absent. Its inputs must be externalized owner observations: identity/topology acceptance, exact current claim/resource/alias authority and revalidation, continuous writable-recovery authority, per-store `AdmissionStabilizationEvidence`, exact checksum-owner comparison, the recovery-produced range-role basis snapshot and source evidence, separate coded-range-authority and recovery `CLEAN` observations, durable session begin, and durable epoch outcome. It must prove no publication/current promotion on unknown or rejected outcomes and must not model payload bytes, operation-slot internals, coded membership, CLEAN capture mechanics, currentization, operator command paths, frontend runtime, or file-backed lease-release semantics.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Scan every payload:** does not prove historical continuity and is outside present-data admission.
- **Use recovery history as physical quiescence:** history can preserve intent, identity, disposition, or uncertainty but cannot observe that every predecessor or unattributed route/effect has completed or ceased for the stable backing resource.
- **Use writer-claim acquisition as stabilization:** exclusion blocks a concurrent current writer but cannot prove that effects from predecessor claims or physical routes have completed or ceased.
- **Use a timestamp or accepted-write watermark:** loses exact current binding, stable-resource/alias identity, and complete physical reachability scope and cannot certify the physical no-late boundary.
- **Let healthy service derive terminality or generic handoff:** duplicates operation-slot, transaction, recovery, and lifecycle authority.
- **Stabilize without continuously holding/revalidating the exact claim/resource binding or without closing physical predecessor reachability:** permits the binding to change or leaves old/unattributed effects capable of reaching the resource.
- **Create a startup-specific release/lifecycle owner:** duplicates existing operation-slot and related release ownership.
- **Treat unresolved CLEAN as operation-release failure:** conflates independent dirty-integrity capture uncertainty with operation release and overblocks unrelated work.
- **Publish first and commit epoch later:** creates an unowned writable interval.
- **Retry unknown claim/session/epoch outcomes automatically:** can create duplicate authority or generation confusion.
- **Treat parity agreement, old `current`, or successful open as current protection:** none supplies the owner-required committed basis transition.

## Risks and mitigations

- A backend may not provide the required physical stabilization profile or may be unable to identify and close every capable predecessor or unattributed physical route. Report `unsupported`, `failed`, or `uncertain`; do not infer write-safe authority.
- A current writer claim may be acquired, including for a new incarnation, while a predecessor, old-kernel/device, DMA, raw, alias, or remapped effect may still be completing. Do not report success until exact scope evidence proves completion or cessation and final binding revalidation succeeds.
- A store, claim, or alias binding may disappear during any later gate. Preserve `uncertain` or fail closed; do not automatically retry or reuse the result. A later attempt must reacquire the exact current binding and obtain fresh evidence with fresh physical scope.
- A frontend may not enforce mixed basis gates. Refuse that publication profile rather than collapsing ranges into one health bit.
- Durable session/epoch acknowledgement may be lost. Preserve prior/proposed evidence and owner next action; never auto-retry.
- Owner target changes may invalidate this packet. Re-read final owner identities and affected dependents before implementation or canonical sync.
